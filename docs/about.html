About

I'm Ezra Crangle. I write Ezra's Notes, a newsletter on value investing. Every quarter, US investment firms with more than $100 million in US-listed stocks file a 13F with the SEC listing what they own. It's public and free, but it sits on EDGAR as raw XML. This site takes the 13Fs of the managers I follow and lays them out quarter by quarter.

The list of managers is mine. The numbers come straight from each filing. The only adjustment is for stock splits, so a 20-for-1 split doesn't look like a big buy.

What a 13F shows, and what it leaves out

It lists long positions in US-listed stocks, including ADRs of foreign companies, plus some options and convertible bonds, as of the last day of the quarter.

Firms have 45 days after the quarter ends to file. By the time a 13F is public, the manager may have sold.

Short positions, cash, bonds, private investments and stocks that only trade outside the US don't appear. A manager with half the fund in Europe shows up here as a smaller, US-only portfolio. Read every page on this site as a partial view.

How the numbers work

Weight is a position's value divided by the total value of that 13F. Because the 13F leaves things out, a 10% weight here can be a smaller share of the real fund.

Held since is the first quarter of the current unbroken run. "Or earlier" means the position was already in the first 13F on file. If a manager sells and buys back, the count starts again.

Changes compare the number of shares with the previous quarter. A change in value alone, from the price moving, doesn't count as a move. When a firm amends a 13F, the site uses the amended version.

Sectors

Every company that files with the SEC has an industry code (SIC) on EDGAR. The site groups those codes into broad sectors, so the source stays the SEC. The codes date from the 1930s and some fit badly: Amazon is "catalog and mail-order retail" and Netflix is "video tape rental". That's why the groups are broad. ETFs and other funds get their own line.

When it updates

The site checks EDGAR several times a day in the weeks when 13Fs are due (the first half of February, May, August and November) and every Monday the rest of the year. Late filings and amendments come in the same way. Nothing already published gets deleted.

Investor Letters
